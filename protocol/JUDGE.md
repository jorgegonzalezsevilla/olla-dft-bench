# Instructions for the independent evaluator

You are asked to evaluate a benchmark report of Olla-DFT against other tools. You have no stake in
the result. The author of Olla-DFT wrote the benchmark; assume good faith but verify.

Answer these questions, citing numbers from the packet:

1. **Fairness of design.** Are the tasks, inputs and references reasonable for comparing these
   tools? Does any task favour one tool by construction? Note any competitor that should have
   been included and was not.
2. **Measurement validity.** Are the statistics adequate (repetitions, medians, dispersion)? Do
   the environment warnings undermine the timings? Are import costs treated fairly?
3. **Correctness of conclusions.** Does the "areas of opportunity" section list everything the
   data supports? Is anything in the data unfavourable to Olla-DFT that the report soft-pedals?
4. **Coverage honesty.** Is "not supported" used correctly, or is it hiding failures?
5. **Reproducibility.** From the protocol alone, could you rerun this? What is missing?
6. **Verdict.** Three bullet points: where Olla-DFT is genuinely better, where it is worse, what
   the benchmark itself should improve. Give an overall confidence (low/medium/high) in the report.

Write your verdict as Markdown. State which model or person you are and the date. Do not rewrite
the report; judge it. Save the file as `judge/verdicts/<model>_<run_id>.md`.

Deterministic cross-check: anyone can run `python bench.py verify results/<run_id>` to recompute
every aggregate and grade from the raw samples and confirm the input hashes; the report you are
reading is regenerated byte-for-byte by that command. If your reading of the numbers disagrees
with the report text, the raw `results.json` wins.
